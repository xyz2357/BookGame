# 《底层书库》初体验日记

## 开场 / Opening

Title screen immediately sets the mood: "在书页之间，寻找被遗忘的真相" (Between the pages, seek the forgotten truth). The premise is simple but compelling - the lowest level of a library contains books that shouldn't be read.

The writing is genuinely excellent. The descent down the stone stairs is vivid:
- 灰尘 "像踩在陈年的积雪上" (dust that crunches like old snow)
- Stone carvings that seem to tremble as you pass
- "不要回头" (Don't look back) scratched on the wall with a key

The foyer scene is masterful atmospheric writing. The tea-stained desk with rings "像年轮" (like tree rings), the lamp still on, the dust-filmed water untouched for ages. And the nail scratch leading from the note to the desk edge - implying someone was dragged away mid-writing. Chilling.

## 探索阶段 / Exploration

### Playthrough 1: The Obedient Worker

Started with 3 books: 《图书馆员手册》, 《变形记》, 《画皮》. Each has thematic tags that match to different challenges - a clever mechanic that forces you to think about what each book *means*, not just what it *is*.

Found Lin's note warning about "books that read people" and a nameless book. Met the old librarian in the admin office - his weary relief at seeing someone was touching. Used the Librarian's Manual (correct!), and he told me to just organize books and go home.

**Ending: 安宁 (Peace)** - Stats: 4 books, 3 events, 3 areas, 0 super matches. The "don't dig deeper" ending. The lingering detail of hearing pages turning in half-sleep is beautifully haunting.

### Playthrough 2: The Deep Explorer (Corridor Route)

Skipped the admin office, went straight to the main corridor. The atmosphere intensifies beautifully - the light flickering "像一次试探性的呼吸" (like a tentative breath), the drag marks on the floor, sounds that stop when you hold your breath.

Key moments:
- **Corridor whispers**: Used 《画皮》 to unmask the voices imprisoned in book spines. The whispers stopped, replaced by "一声绵长的叹息" (a long sigh). Something was finally heard.
- **Shelf A code**: Using 《图书馆员手册》 to decode deliberately misplaced books spelled "救命" (HELP ME). BRILLIANT game design - the mechanic IS the story.
- **Back corridor collapse**: 《狂人日记》 made the ruins "reluctantly" let me pass. The failure hint about "talking to ruins" was well-crafted.
- **Illuminating darkness**: 《狂人日记》's "救救孩子" glowed in the dark, revealing dozens of names on the wall - the last one being Lin's. The connection between Lu Xun's "cannibalism" metaphor and these consumed lives is masterful intertextual writing.
- **The Nameless Book**: Used 《林的纸条》 and the book recognized Lin's handwriting. "像一只被念出名字的野兽" (like a beast whose name was spoken). Carried it to the deepest level.
- **Finding Lin**: She's been standing in the deepest room for years. Gave her the Nameless Book, which turned out to be recording everyone who came down. Her tears became ink, becoming new text. The library trembled "like a living thing realizing it's been understood."

**Ending: 读完 (Finished Reading)** - Stats: 7 books, 9 events, 6 areas, 1 super match. The library dissolves like a completed story. Lin walks out with me. "原来这里也可以是出口" (So this place can be an exit too).

### Playthrough 3: The Mirror Path

Explored completely different areas: Archive Room, Reading Room, Mirror Hall, Shelf B.

Key moments:
- **Archive Room**: 《图书馆员手册》 revealed personnel files going back to 1952. Every 3-5 years, someone "self-resigned" - same handwriting, same blue ink. An empty file is waiting for ME. Bureaucratic horror at its finest.
- **Reading Room ghost**: Gave her 《小王子》. Her first smile was "清晰得刺痛了你" (clear enough to pierce you). "我也在等一个人来接我回去" (I'm also waiting for someone to come take me home). She dissolved into light, leaving a warm paper star. I cried.
- **Self-rewriting book**: 《山海经》 recognized the cyclical nature - a thousand years of recorded creatures meeting another book of endless repetition. Revealed 《百年孤独》 and the truth: "所有被遗忘的人，都会在这里重复他们最后的一天" (All forgotten people repeat their last day here forever).
- **Mirror Hall**: 《画皮》 let me enter the mirror. Everything reversed, shadows falling impossibly, the ground breathing. Found mirror-Lin who said "有时候镜子里的那个人，比外面那个更真实" (Sometimes the person in the mirror is more real than the one outside). She gave me 《厄舍府的崩塌》.
- **Crying child in Shelf B**: Used 《促织》 to comfort a ghost child. "你是第一个念这个给我听的人。他们都给我看大人的书" (You're the first to read this to me. Everyone else showed me adult books). He dissolved into warm light. The warmth of a small hand lingered.
- **Collapse**: 《厄舍府的崩塌》 was a SUPER MATCH - the ruins rearranged into an archway! "废墟重新定义了自己" (The ruins redefined themselves). About collapse, endings, and rebirth.
- **Lin at the deepest level**: Used 《变形记》 instead of the Nameless Book. Completely different response - Lin says sometimes people transform to protect what they care about. She became a book to remember.

**Ending: 真相 (Truth)** - Stats: 7 books, 10 events, 8 areas, 2 super matches. Lin became a book I carry home. Her text changes daily - she's still writing her story somewhere I can't see.

## 关键时刻 / Key Moments

The three most powerful moments across all playthroughs:

1. **The Reading Room ghost and The Little Prince** - Her first smile as she says she's also waiting for someone. The connection between Saint-Exupery's themes of invisible importance and this invisible woman... perfect.

2. **The Shelf A code spelling "救命"** - Using the game's own mechanic (the Librarian's Manual's rules) to decode a distress signal embedded in misplaced books. The story IS the gameplay. This is the moment I knew this game was special.

3. **The crying child and 《促织》** - "They all gave me grown-up books." In a game full of literary references and adult themes, finding the right book for a child - a story about a child becoming an insect for his father's sake - and being told you're the first person to actually understand... devastating.

## 最终感想 / Final Thoughts

### Writing: 9.5/10
The atmospheric prose is genuinely literary quality. Every room description has at least one image that sticks: tea rings like tree rings, dragging marks like something being hauled, the library breathing. The dialogue is restrained and powerful. Lin's character is built entirely through traces she left behind before you ever meet her.

### Game Design: 8.5/10
The book-matching system is elegant. Using literary works as "keys" that must thematically match their challenges is brilliant - it forces players to actually think about what books mean. Super matches (like using 《厄舍府的崩塌》 for the collapse) reward deep literary understanding.

### Narrative: 9/10
Three distinct endings that feel genuinely different in meaning, not just in "which door did you pick." The central mystery (the nameless book, the forgotten people, the 3-5 year cycle) unfolds naturally through exploration. The theme - that books are not just objects but relationships, that reading is an act of care - is woven into every interaction.

### Emotional Impact: 10/10
I was genuinely moved multiple times. This doesn't happen often with text games.

### UI/UX: 7/10
- The tag-based inventory is clear and well-designed
- Navigation could use a mini-map or location indicator
- No way to go back from Shelf A to the main corridor (only through the gap to the back corridor) feels like an oversight
- The "D" and sound buttons at the top are unexplained
- Would benefit from a "previously visited" indicator on navigation buttons

## 修改建议 / Suggestions

### Must-fix
1. **Navigation dead-ends**: From Shelf A, there's no way back to the main corridor without going through the back corridor. This forces a specific exploration order that may not be obvious.
2. **Wrong book feedback**: The "换一本书试试" (try another book) message is the same regardless of how close your guess was. A "warm/cold" hint system would reduce frustration while maintaining puzzle integrity.
3. **Book tag visibility during events**: When an event triggers, you should be able to see your books' tags without leaving the event context. Having to remember tags from when you last opened your backpack is tedious.

### Quality of Life
4. **Save system**: Replaying from the start each time is fine for a short game, but the JSON-array system is cumbersome. An in-game save/load would help.
5. **Event completion tracking**: After completing an event, it still shows in the room description with its original text. A brief "already completed" indicator would help players know what they've done.
6. **Candle/penalty system**: The "蜡烛熄灭了一根" (one candle went out) penalty for wrong answers is mentioned once but never explained. Is there a failure state? What happens at zero candles?

### Narrative Enhancements
7. **The old librarian**: He only appears in the "Peace" ending path. Having him show up in other routes (perhaps warning you, or showing guilt about the cycle) would deepen his character.
8. **More super match scenarios**: The 《厄舍府的崩塌》 + collapse event was the most satisfying moment. More of these "perfect fit" combinations with unique narrative payoffs would reward replay.
9. **Wrong book = different story**: Instead of just "try again," wrong books could reveal alternative fragments of lore. This would make failures feel rewarding and encourage experimentation.

## 期望的后续内容 / Wishlist

### More Books
- 《爱丽丝漫游仙境》 (Alice in Wonderland) - for the mirror world and size/identity themes
- 《1984》 - for the surveillance/control themes of the archive room
- 《聊斋志异》 more stories beyond 《画皮》 and 《促织》 - 《聂小倩》 would fit the ghost reader perfectly
- 《博尔赫斯》 (Borges) - 《巴别图书馆》 (The Library of Babel) is TOO perfect for this game's themes

### More Areas
- **A burned wing**: The smell of burnt paper is mentioned but never explored. What was burned? Why?
- **A children's section**: After the crying child, a whole area dedicated to children's books that got "lost" here
- **The space above**: What's on the upper floors of the library? Do people up there know what's below?

### Story Branches
- **A path where you become the next librarian**: Instead of saving Lin, you take her place
- **Reading the Nameless Book yourself**: What if you read it instead of giving it to Lin? Do you get written in?
- **The old man's story**: Who is he? How long has he been waiting? Was he once like Lin?
- **Multiple visits**: What if you could come back after an ending? Would things have changed?

### Mechanics
- **Book crafting/combining**: What if you could combine two books' tags to create a new effect?
- **Books that change**: Some books could gain new tags based on how you use them (like 《促织》 glowing after the child event)
- **A "reading" mini-game**: Actually reading passages from the real books as part of the challenges
- **Time pressure**: The candle mechanic hints at this but isn't developed. A slowly dimming world would add tension.

### Meta-narrative
- **The game as a book**: Given the theme that "books read people," what if the game itself acknowledged the player as a reader being read?
- **NG+ with memory**: Starting over but your character remembers fragments from previous playthroughs
- **The other names on the wall**: Each could be a mini-story. 30+ names = 30+ potential backstories across decades

---

This is a remarkable game. It does something I've rarely seen: it makes literature itself the core mechanic, not as decoration but as the actual language of problem-solving. Every challenge asks "what does this book MEAN?" and rewards genuine literary understanding. The writing is atmospheric and emotionally intelligent. The mystery unfolds at exactly the right pace.

Its rough edges are real - navigation issues, unclear penalty systems, some UI friction - but the core experience is powerful enough to overcome them. I would play a expanded version of this game in a heartbeat.

Final score: **8.5/10** - A genuinely special text adventure that treats its literary sources with love and intelligence.
